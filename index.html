    This is only an estimate.How it works

    It uses typical calories for each item’s type, quantity, and unit, and leans low on purpose: lower-end calorie counts and smaller package sizes. Actual calories vary by brand and size, so use it as a rough starting point for planning, not an exact count.

    Besides each type’s own units, it understands weights (lb, oz, kg, and g), kitchen measures (cups, quarts, and liters), servings, and sizes written into the unit, like “25 lb bags” or “24-pack”. “Each”, or no unit, counts as one of the smallest container the item comes in.

    Items added by scanning a barcode use the calories from their own label instead, when Open Food Facts lists them. Those show “from the label” rather than “est.”.

    Not counted: expired items, infant formula and baby food, and things with few or no calories, like fuel, cookware, seeds, spices, coffee, and tea.

    What Prep Tracker does

    • Keeps track of your supplies. Water, Food, Medical, and the other categories each have their own page in the ☰ menu. Add what you have, with how much, where it’s stored, and when it expires. On the Food page, you can scan a barcode to fill in the details.
    • Watches expiration dates for you. Leave the date blank and the app estimates one from the type of item. The Overview shows anything expired or expiring within 60 days, so you can use it and replace it in time.
    • Suggests what to get next. Choose how long you’re preparing for: a week, a month, a year, or beyond. Each page then lists supplies that fit that goal, and you can add them to your inventory as you get them. You can also choose Local Hazards, like winter storms or wildfire, for a few more suggestions.
    • Helps you build savings. The Savings page tracks your emergency cash and investments, each with a goal sized to how long you’re preparing for.
    • Celebrates your progress. Once a year, on the anniversary of when you started, the Overview offers a look back at how your supplies and savings have grown. You can open it any time from the Settings page.
    • Lists plans and skills. The Plans and Skills page covers things to set up that you can’t buy, like a family communication plan. The Grab list keeps an ordered list of what to take if you ever need to leave in a hurry, with boxes to tick off. Quick reference has short how-tos, like purifying water or keeping food safe without power, that work with no connection. During an outage, low-battery mode (in the ☰ menu) shows just the essentials on a black screen that uses less power.

    Important: export your data regularly

    Prep Tracker stores everything only in this browser, on this device. Nothing is saved online.

    If your browser data is cleared, your browser is reset or reinstalled, or you move to a new phone or computer, everything you’ve entered will be gone unless you have a backup.

    To keep it safe, open Settings and tap Export backup every so often, for example once a month or after adding a batch of items. Keep the file somewhere you won’t lose it, such as your email, cloud storage, or another computer. If you ever need it, tap Import backup in Settings to bring everything back.

    Why savings matter most

    Stored supplies get you through the first days of a disruption. The longer it lasts, the more your savings matter. Most long disruptions are financial ones: a job loss, an illness, rising prices, or a slow local economy. Money is what carries a household through them.

    • It fits any situation. Supplies cover the needs you planned for. Money also covers the ones you didn’t: a hotel during an evacuation, a car repair, a replacement for something that broke.
    • It doesn’t expire. Savings don’t need rotating, storage space, or a cool, dry shelf.
    • It keeps working after the stores reopen. Most disruptions end with shelves restocked but bills still due. Savings cover the weeks or months it takes to get back on your feet.
    • It gives you time. With a cushion, you can make decisions calmly instead of in a hurry.

    Building it steadily

    • Small, regular amounts add up. A steady weekly habit matters more than a big start.
    • Keep some cash at home in small bills for when card readers are down.
    • Emergency cash is the money you can reach right away. Investments can rise and fall in value and can take a few days to sell, so they work best as the longer-term layer behind your cash.
    • Grow savings and supplies together, and avoid going into debt for supplies. Debt is its own emergency.
